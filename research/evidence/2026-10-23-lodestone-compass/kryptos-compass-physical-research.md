# Kryptos: physical compass, lodestone and orientation evidence

Research date: 23 October 2026. Scope: public-source research and inspection only. No cipher, magnetic-model, astronomical, or alignment hypothesis was tested; no one was contacted and no restricted site was accessed.

## Bottom line

The strongest evidence establishes an engraved compass rose on a granite slab at the New Headquarters Building entrance, with a separate nearby lodestone, and a carved needle intended to point toward that stone. It does **not** establish a survey-grade true bearing of 220°, 247.5°, or any other exact value. The widely repeated bearings mix different physical axes, differently reliable testimony, and diagram interpretations.

The entrance installation and the main copper screen are separate parts of Kryptos on opposite sides of the New Headquarters Building. The public aerial shows the building block between the entrance approach and the courtyard. They are not neighboring objects facing each other across the courtyard. A true compass-to-screen bearing or distance cannot safely be supplied from the evidence inspected here.

An important counterweight to the 2003 “north points roughly 220°” report is the 2005 Dunin/Wilson recollection that the rose was intended to point north, while its needle was intended to point to the lodestone. That does not settle the actual installed alignment, but makes it particularly unsafe to equate engraved N, engraved needle and a real magnetic compass reading.

## 1. Five quantities that must stay separate

1. **Engraved N axis:** the line from the rose's center through its N marking. It is a fixed artistic marking, not a moving instrument.
2. **Engraved needle axis:** the long, double-ended needle-like figure cut into the slab. It is visibly not collinear with the engraved N–S axis. A line has two opposite directions; assigning its north-seeking tip requires independent evidence.
3. **Rose-center-to-lodestone vector:** the direction from a defined point on the engraving to a defined point on the nearby rock. Photographic apparent alignment is not automatically an exact survey bearing, and the rock's center is not necessarily its magnetic pole.
4. **Geographical true north:** an external geodetic direction. It cannot be recovered simply by trusting the engraved N label, rotating a photograph upright, or using the top of a screenshot as north.
5. **An actual magnetized instrument needle:** a reading at a specific three-dimensional position and time, affected by the local field, the stone, surrounding ferrous material and instrument behavior. A claim that every passing compass points WSW is not established by one carved direction.

These distinctions are analytical definitions, not proposed mechanisms for K4. ENE and WSW are exact opposites in the standard 16-point nomenclature (67.5° and 247.5°). That geometrical fact does not prove the installed artwork embodies those geographical bearings. A 220° azimuth is near southwest (225°), not exact WSW (247.5°); SSW is 202.5°. Never “correct” an uncertain observed value to 247.5° because it suits an ENE hypothesis.

## 2. Location, relationship to the copper screen, and physical appearance

### Official institutional description

The CIA's current page locates the entrance components along the walkway from the parking deck to NHB, and separately describes the courtyard screen and pools. It explicitly identifies a naturally magnetized rock co-located with a navigational compass rose. The page supplies an official close-up photograph, but no survey bearings, instrument readings, distance to the copper screen, capture date, or north reference. Its image alt text describes a southwest-pointing compass; that is not equivalent to a measured geographical azimuth. [CIA, “Kryptos” Sculpture, undated current page](https://www.cia.gov/legacy/headquarters/kryptos-sculpture/).

### Aerial evidence

Elonka Dunin's page was created 16 October 2003 and notes a 10 November 2003 revision following an interview with Sanborn. It places the screen in the northwest courtyard/plaza, and the entrance rose somewhere south of the entrance walkway while explicitly acknowledging uncertain exact placement. The inspected whole-site image shows the broad NHB block between entrance and courtyard. Thus “opposite sides of NHB” is well-supported at descriptive scale. A west/southwest relationship is apparent in the usual published map presentation, but its crop alone lacks sufficient geodetic metadata for a precise bearing or separation. [Explanatory page](https://www.elonka.com/kryptos/KryptosAerial.html); [whole-site aerial](https://www.elonka.com/kryptos/KryptosAerial.jpg); [entrance enlargement](https://www.elonka.com/kryptos/AerialImages/WalkwayEnlarged.jpg).

### What the photographs directly show

Jim Gillogly's gallery identifies its photographs as copyright 1999; Chris Hanson's old site describes the visit as November 1999. The gallery calls this a compass-inscribed slab outside the building and provides two views. The first distinguishes cardinal markings, the long needle and a separate rock beyond the rose; the second supplies broader physical context. The CIA photograph independently shows the rock outside the engraved circle, not at its center. These are reliable visual observations of form and adjacency, not angle measurements. [Gillogly gallery](https://www.voynich.net/Kryptos/); [compass1](https://voynich.net/Kryptos/full/compass1.jpg); [compass2](https://voynich.net/Kryptos/full/compass2.jpg); [CIA photograph](https://www.cia.gov/static/9ff42ea035bb1e7aba9591db8beeaefb/63012/kryptos_sculpture_2_lg.jpg).

The 1999 and CIA photos show the same basic configuration. The CIA photograph's modern hosting location does not establish a recent capture date, or continuous unchanged placement since 1990. No original 1990 as-built survey or date-stamped installation photograph of this alignment was located.

### NSA document: provenance and crucial limitation

The PDF is DOCID 4112086, released 14 April 2014 under FOIA case 62223. Its second physical PDF page shows an 18 November 1991 memorandum, from George C. Dalmas according to the OCR (surname is imperfectly reproduced), addressed to “CRYPTO Novices Everywhere.” The fourth physical PDF page is internally headed page 3. The writer lists four things to investigate as clues: screen, lodestone/compass, Morse and benches. This is the writer's assessment, not a direct Sanborn statement that those four objects are cryptographic keys. The memo also entertains now-obsolete five-part/paragraph ideas, reinforcing its status as contemporary working investigation rather than authoritative final design documentation. [Original scan](https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/cia-kryptos-sculpture/doc_3.pdf).

**Visual annotation note:** On physical PDF page 4, beneath the introductory compass-point-south clause, visual inspection finds an unidentified handwritten correction resembling “no, [approximately?] 230°–240°.” The exact symbol is not reliably legible. This is retained for independent checking, not promoted to a measured orientation or assigned to engraved N. The uncertain reading should be checked against the original scan rather than quoted as an established bearing. Firecrawl's OCR displaced it into a heading, illustrating why text extraction alone is unsafe here.

### October 2005 recollections: agreement and differences

The event index identifies the Sanborn lunch as 29 October 2005 and attributes Hanson's notes to Yahoo message 3842 plus the former Kryptos wiki. Hanson's exact short sentence is: “The compass needle is intended to point to the lodestone.” His next observation says Sanborn seemed unaware of additional supposed targets such as the water tower or copper screen. [Event/source index](https://www.thekryptosproject.com/events/dinners/2005.php); [Hanson PDF](https://www.thekryptosproject.com/events/dinners/pdfs/2005/2005_hanson.pdf).

Dunin/Wilson's separate raw notes say the rose was intended north, with a tentative mention of magnetic variation; they agree on needle-to-lodestone intent, but say Sanborn declined to answer about an additional target. These accounts should not be blended into a stronger categorical denial of every other alignment. They support intended local pointing, while leaving actual orientation and additional meaning unresolved. [Dunin/Wilson PDF, physical page 2](https://www.thekryptosproject.com/events/dinners/pdfs/2005/2005_dunin.pdf).

### A second official-hosted description

Exa retrieved the indexed text of CIA FOIA record C06498615, an Intellipedia printout dated 23 September 2014 (release metadata July 2017). Its third page distinguishes the engraved needle from magnetic north, explicitly saying the former points at the lodestone. This is useful institutional corroboration, but it is a compilation with secondary references and not a field log. The current live URL redirected to the CIA reading-room homepage in Firecrawl and web fetches, so provenance is **indexed text retrieved by Exa, not a currently rendered original PDF**. [CIA record URL](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/06498615).

## 4. Schematics, original artwork and modern reconstructions

### Monet Friedrich diagrams

Kryptosfan's Morse page attributes two installation sketches to Monet Friedrich and photos to Gillogly. The image paths are under May 2009; comments establish public circulation by July 2009, but neither establishes the drawings' original creation date. The original SFU compass-analysis page now returns 404. The inspected south/compass schematic depicts the carved needle relative to cardinal letters and includes a separate miniature orientation sketch. It is not an as-built survey and supplies no instrument, date or angular uncertainty. [Reproduction page](https://kryptosfan.wordpress.com/morse-code/); [south schematic](https://kryptosfan.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2009/05/morse-south.gif); [unavailable original analytical page](https://www.sfu.ca/~nicolea/kryptos/CompassStrataLinearAberationsAndDYAHR.htm).

Numberworld's October 2022 angle claim is expressly derived after discussing these drawings, not from a visit. Its July 2020 post already links the visual compass to ENE/WSW and admits uncertainty about which end represents north. Therefore the ENE/WSW observation predates current 2026 solution sites, but its historical existence does not validate geographical precision. [2020](https://www.numberworld.blog/2020/07/kryptos-cipher-part-4.html); [2022](https://www.numberworld.blog/2022/10/kryptos-cipher-part-5.html).

### Collector's photograph of compass studies

Kryptosbot says it photographed publicly available material in the Smithsonian's Jim Sanborn papers in March 2026. Its IMG_1518 shows three hand-drawn roses on graph paper. Direct inspection confirms N/S/E/W labels and long needles well away from north, extending into NE/SW quadrants and closer to E/W than N/S. The visible page has no azimuth numbers, site labels or date. Its attribution to Sanborn's papers is the collector's claim; no box/folder/item identifier is provided for this image. Even authenticated design studies would not establish the installed geographic bearing. [Collection page](https://www.kryptosbot.com/archive/); [image](https://www.kryptosbot.com/static/archive/IMG_1518.jpg); [linked Smithsonian collection](https://www.aaa.si.edu/collections/jim-sanborn-papers-22298).

Do not relabel these as surveyed installation plans, rehost the collector's image, or infer an exact 247.5° from an oblique photograph of a hand drawing.

### Models are not exact substitutes

Hanson's 2005 notes describe differences between Sanborn's miniature models and the actual sculpture: reversed tableau orientation in one, different font/spacing, missing details, and unfastened model pieces. They also record that Sanborn had not independently checked the entire installed stone arrangement from above. Thus a maquette or modern 3D reconstruction needs a provenance and fidelity audit before being used for metric geometry. [Hanson PDF](https://www.thekryptosproject.com/events/dinners/pdfs/2005/2005_hanson.pdf).

## 5. Bench orientation, dimensions and maps

### Benches

The 1991 memo describes two **wooden** benches on opposing sides of a lily pond, one facing north and the other south, with no special markings. It gives neither bearings nor measurement procedure. Elonka's wish list separately asks about **stone** benches behind the screen. These should not automatically be assumed to identify the same pair of objects. There is no publicly documented survey of bench long axes, seat-facing directions, or historical relocation in the inspected sources. [NSA page 4](https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/cia-kryptos-sculpture/doc_3.pdf); [wish list](https://www.elonka.com/kryptos/wishlist.html).

### Published dimensions

Elonka's aerial page gives overall artwork-group dimensions: plaza 20 ft × 10 ft × 12 ft; lawn 60 ft × 12 ft × 3 ft; entrance 150 ft × 12 ft × 3 ft. Its reproduced Sanborn description specifies 12-inch-thick granite slabs. These are descriptive artwork dimensions, not a precise plan for compass-circle diameter, rose-to-rock separation or screen-to-rose distance. The 1991 memo separately describes two 8 ft × 8 ft copper sheets of half-inch thickness and a 12-foot petrified trunk. Do not silently reconcile this coarse description with the actual four-panel construction or treat it as a modern survey. [Elonka aerial](https://www.elonka.com/kryptos/KryptosAerial.html); [NSA page 4](https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/cia-kryptos-sculpture/doc_3.pdf).

### Mapping methods already attempted

Chris “Xenon” Hanson described plotting the K2 coordinates in Visual Nature Studio 2 against a USGS-contract color orthophoto of 1 ft/pixel, comparing NAD27 with NAD83/WGS84. He marked north-up and estimated roughly metre-scale plot accuracy. His assumptions about the original datum and GPS origin are explicitly his reasoning, not Sanborn's metadata; the page even labels the coordinate passage “Part 3,” which is not today's K2 nomenclature. This is historical geospatial investigation, not a survey of the rose's N axis. Retrieved through Exa's index; live HTTPS page returned 404. [Hanson coordinates page](http://kryptos.arcticus.com/coords.html).

Lann's later pages show hand-registered compass/aerial overlays and strata/GPS lines, explicitly approximate. No photogrammetric calibration is supplied. [Compass overlay](https://www.thekryptosproject.com/tjp/observations/compass.php); [line comparisons](https://www.thekryptosproject.com/tjp/observations/lines.php).

## 6. Sun and shadow investigations are longstanding

The general idea is demonstrably not novel:

- **18 October 2002, firsthand observation:** Elonka reports afternoon sunlight producing readable light-letter projections and, in other curved areas, narrow bars. She also traced letters appearing on the white rock to reflected sunlight from NHB windows rather than a direct ray from the apparent sun position. She explicitly says she did not have time to see the entrance components. This is observed light behavior, not evidence of a decoded shadow cipher. [Elonka's visit account on main page](https://www.elonka.com/kryptos/index.html).
- **2003–2009, requested measurements:** the wish list was created 14 December 2003 and last modified 16 February 2009. It asks for morning/noon/late-afternoon clear-day photos, reflected letter projections, possible night-light effects and detailed dimensions for 3D artists. This documents methods proposed and missing evidence, not completed calibrated experiments. [Wish list](https://www.elonka.com/kryptos/wishlist.html).
- **2003 mapping:** the aerial page proposes comparing lamp-post shadows across ground and aerial photographs to locate slabs. That is an explicit image-registration idea, separate from a shadow-based cipher solution. [Aerial page](https://www.elonka.com/kryptos/KryptosAerial.html).
- **2005 modeling discussions:** Dunin/Wilson notes record Sanborn's surprise that the pool's rotation had not been included in a 3D model, and discussion of light inside an experimental cylindrical arrangement. Hanson discusses point-light projection from small maquettes. Neither is a documented solar-azimuth solution. [Dunin/Wilson](https://www.thekryptosproject.com/events/dinners/pdfs/2005/2005_dunin.pdf); [Hanson](https://www.thekryptosproject.com/events/dinners/pdfs/2005/2005_hanson.pdf).
- **20 April 2006, contemporary journalism:** Wired identifies Chris Hanson's model of the CIA building complex and landscaped grounds as an effort to study the surroundings for clues. It supplies no angular or temporal validation of a particular shadow alignment. [“Typo Confounds Kryptos Sleuths”](https://www.wired.com/2006/04/typo-confounds-kryptos-sleuths/).

Search also surfaced current 2026 shadow-tip preprints and optical forum proposals. These are proof of continued speculation, not independent verification. They were not used to set an alignment, astronomical target, or test result in this report.

## 7. Audit of new source claims supplied during the investigation

### SolveKryptos

The about page puts the rose in the courtyard near the screen and the lodestone at its center. Those statements conflict with the CIA location description and inspected photos. It supplies no observer, field date, instrument, position, reading, or primary citation for reported WSW needle deflection. Its local-reference-frame discussion is author interpretation, not an attributed Sanborn quotation. Its field guide converts exact 67.5° and 247.5° into numerical seeds for a proposed reconstruction; that is not a measurement report. Treat it as a hypothesis source, not authoritative physical evidence. [About](https://solvekryptos.com/about); [field guide](https://solvekryptos.com/fieldguide).

### Academia/JVE paper

The discovered paper, attributed to Josie O Magee Bélanger, describes an independent investigation on 16 June 2026. Its publicly scraped page yielded the abstract, not the full physical-evidence section. Search text claims a magnetic needle toward WSW at approximately 220° and attributes a local-reference-frame philosophy to Sanborn; this is internally inconsistent in direction and unverified attribution. Without the relevant full text and primary citation, do not quote it as measurement or artist testimony. [Paper landing page](https://www.academia.edu/169821945/Kryptos_K4_A_Structural_Investigation_The_Compass_Rose_the_Cold_War_and_the_Limits_of_Public_Cryptanalysis).

### Other supplied pages

Frank Prins's 7 May 2024 essay cautiously suggests SHADOW FORCES might relate to compass/lodestone, but supplies no physical bearing or measurement. Paradigm's history page describes Sanborn's earlier magnetism-related installations and reproduces archival images, but the inspected history text does not supply a surveyed rose orientation. Their thematic relevance should not be upgraded to metric evidence. [Prins](https://prinsfrank.nl/2024/05/07/Kryptos-cypher-does-RQ-mean-e-for-k4); [Paradigm history](https://www.paradigm.xyz/kryptos/history).

When quoting decoded inscriptions, preserve IQLUSION and UNDERGRUUND in literal transcriptions. Any corrected spelling belongs in an explicitly normalized paraphrase. This report did not re-transcribe all K0–K3 text or test semantic links.

## 9. Open measurement gaps before any later testing

The necessary missing record is not another attractive direction name. It is an independently sourced coordinate system and measurement provenance:

- As-built north-referenced positions of the rose center, N/S/E/W radial lines, both needle tips, and defined points on the stone.
- Azimuth uncertainty, survey method, datum and epoch; explicit distinction among true, grid and magnetic north.
- Rose diameter, slab tilt/elevation and camera calibration sufficient to rectify photographs without assuming a circle viewed frontally.
- Original 1990 versus later positions, including maintenance or movable-stone records; modern-looking web hosting is not a capture date.
- If physical magnetic behavior becomes relevant later: documented instrument readings with exact measurement positions/heights and dates. This report performs no such model.
- For any later light study: timestamp/time zone, sun unobstructed versus reflected illumination, camera pose, geometry/height uncertainties, seasonal vegetation and building-window changes.
- Bench identity, material, seat-facing direction versus long-axis direction and changes over time.
- Smithsonian box/folder/item identifiers and dates for compass sketches, and evidence they were intended for Kryptos rather than another lodestone installation.

Without these, any future test must label bearings as uncertain input ranges or diagram-relative directions rather than surveyed constants. Do not infer novelty: light projections, compass alignments and 3D reconstructions have documented histories.

## 10. Coverage and retrieval limitations

- This investigation used 11 focused Firecrawl search queries and 3 Exa searches. Exa was requested for 10, 8 and 7 result slots, **25 requested slots, not 25 independent sources**. Results included duplicates and unrelated hits.
- 21 distinct relevant webpages/PDFs were substantively inspected through Firecrawl or an alternate rendered page, plus 2 historical records recovered via Exa indexed text (CIA Intellipedia and Hanson's coordinate analysis). Some were partial: the NSA PDF was text-parsed for only its first 5 of 13 pages, with physical pages 2 and 4 independently rendered; the Academia landing page exposed only the abstract/research description, not the full paper.
- Original SFU analysis and live Hanson coordinate page were unavailable; CIA FOIA live record redirected to homepage. Those failures were not interpreted as absence of historical content. The Exa-indexed copies are clearly labeled above.
- No survey-grade compass bearing, exact bench survey, or measured real-compass deflection record was found in this bounded search. That is not a proof none exists elsewhere or in inaccessible records.
- No copyrighted collector image or full source scrape is included in this dossier. Public source links identify the originals.
