# Kryptos: the lodestone, compass rose, and Sanborn's earlier magnetic art

Research date: 23 October 2026. This dossier concerns source history and artist intent. It does not test a cipher, fit a bearing, or treat an artistic association as a K4 decoding rule.

## Principal findings

1. **The lodestone and carved compass rose belong to the entrance portion of Kryptos, not to the main copper screen in the courtyard.** A CIA employee bulletin dated 17 May 1989 already describes a compass rose inscribed on an entrance construction with a piece of lodestone. The courtyard is a separate, connected part of the commission. [S01, S04]
2. **The original artist explanation survives in a released CIA record.** In a signed letter dated 15 December 1989, Sanborn says the entrance lodestone was naturally magnetized by lightning and “refers to ancient navigational compasses.” This is much stronger evidence than a later solver's interpretation. The retrieved copy is a cleaned facsimile on a researcher's website, not a CIA-hosted original; its date, signature, document identifier, and release markings are visible. [S02]
3. **There are two different kinds of compass to keep separate.** Sanborn used suspended, physically movable compass needles in earlier installations. Kryptos has an inscribed compass rose with a depicted needle. A magnetic rock can affect a separate magnetic compass brought nearby, but it cannot rotate a carved stone design. No retrieved original commission record specifies a measured west-southwest deflection of a visitor's compass. [S01, S08, S17]
4. **Magnetism is an established Sanborn subject, not an invention of later Kryptos solvers.** His earlier installations made otherwise invisible forces perceptible. His 2005 account connects that work to Kryptos, but does not identify the entrance compass as a confirmed K4 mechanism. [S06, S07, S08]
5. **Several similarly described works have been conflated.** The 1982 Artists Space exhibition, the artist's separately listed 1985 Hirshhorn installation, the 1987 Arlington public work, and Bethesda's Find the Lodestone are not interchangeable installations. Hidden Under the Three Events is another Bethesda work. [S08–S15]
6. **Navigation has direct original support; astrology does not have the same status.** Astrology appears in a Bethesda institution's description of Hidden Under the Three Events and in journalism about solver approaches. Neither occurrence establishes an astrological program for Kryptos. Shadows are independently documented in Sanborn's art, but that is not evidence of a particular solar or astronomical K4 procedure. [S07, S13, S17]

## 1. The original Kryptos evidence

### A. The 17 May 1989 CIA employee bulletin

**Source and provenance:** CIA Employee Bulletin, “Sculpture for the New Headquarters Building,” released record C00042166. The inspected one-page image carries the CIA emblem, the date, a January 1993 release marking, and a later image-cleaning watermark. The facsimile is hosted by The Kryptos Project. Its content should be attributed to the CIA bulletin, with the hosting and cleanup disclosed. [S01]

The bulletin says the Director of Central Intelligence approved Sanborn's proposal after a June 1988 announcement. It places the two-part work at the **west entrance to the New Headquarters Building** and between the building and cafeteria. Two granite-and-copper constructions were planned to flank the walkway from the parking deck. One would contain an inscribed compass rose and a piece of lodestone. The main copper screen and petrified tree are described separately in the northwest courtyard.

Its materials list includes polished red granite, quartz, copperplates, lodestone, and petrified wood. Its declared theme is the accrual of information, represented through both natural processes and written forms. This is contemporary evidence for the intended entrance arrangement, not merely a present-day reconstruction.

**What it does not establish:** the precise compass bearing, the rock's field strength or polarity, a rotating installed needle, a specific buried magnet, or a K4 algorithm.

### B. Sanborn's signed explanation, 15 December 1989

**Source and provenance:** “To Agency Employees,” signed Jim Sanborn, released record C00042168, two pages. Both pages were visually inspected. The first page shows the date and release marking; the second shows Sanborn's signature and a CIA/Public Affairs fax header. The online copy has a cleaning watermark. [S02]

This resolves the provenance problem of the commonly circulated explanatory letter: a dated facsimile is available, not just an unattributed web transcription. It is nevertheless a third-party-hosted copy of a released government record, rather than a freshly retrieved original from CIA's own repository.

Sanborn describes the stonework as a natural framework recalling the site's earlier stone outcrops. Layering evokes pages; geological material preserves hidden information. The entrance and courtyard copper texts progress from simpler to more complex codes. On page 2, he identifies the entrance rock as lodestone, explains its natural magnetization by lightning, and gives the navigational association quoted above. The letter also discusses water and petrified wood. It contains no explicit compass bearing, astronomical timetable, astrological system, or instruction to use magnetic declination in K4.

The letter says the project was approximately two-thirds complete. Consequently, it documents the design and the artist's explanation during construction, not an independent measurement of every as-built feature.

**Independent early transmission:** The Cryptogram, November–December 1991, printed page 8, reproduces the explanation with its 15 December 1989 date and credits the Naval Cryptologic Veterans Association. This predates the later web transcriptions. [S03]

### C. Current official descriptions and chronology

CIA's present public page retains the entrance/courtyard distinction and associates the lodestone with a navigational compass rose. GSA catalogs Kryptos as a 1990 environmental artwork commissioned through Art in Architecture and gives its dimensions as varying. [S04, S05]

There is a **dedication-date inconsistency worth preserving**. CIA's current page says 3 November 1990. The released copy of William Webster's dedication remarks is labeled 5 November 1990, and an archival-program caption in Paradigm's gallery also gives 5 November. This dossier therefore uses “1990” for completion/dedication rather than silently choosing between conflicting exact dates. The artist's website labels the work 1989, which may describe the work's production date rather than the ceremony. [S04, S09, S22, S24]

### What can be said about Find the Lodestone's date?

The accessible Smithsonian inventory is a Save Outdoor Sculpture! Maryland survey record associated with **1993**, not proof that the sculpture was created in 1993. Its identifier is **IAS MD000204**. The contemporary Bethesda organization does not supply a creation year. Elonka Dunin's secondary pages give 1985 or 1985–86; one paired-work page gives two sets of dimensions without a sufficiently clear assignment to each sculpture. Those numbers should not be promoted into secure object measurements. [S11, S12, S14]

The Smithsonian finding aid independently identifies **Artery Plaza project records, 1984–1986, Box 3, Folder 9**. That is a documentary date range, not necessarily the installation date. The folder is the best identified next source for resolving the discrepancy. [S16]

### Arlington measurements and compass observations are not CIA measurements

Elonka's Arlington page reproduces a purported Smithsonian description and reports later compass observations by Chris “Xenon” Hanson. These concern Arlington's Invisible Forces. The page also reports a conjecture about an additional buried lodestone and a denial attributed to Sanborn. None of that establishes a second magnet, magnetic bearing, or field configuration at CIA headquarters. Its older street address differs from the county's present listing. [S10, S15]

## 3. Artist intent: evidence with an attributable speaker

### Sanborn interviewed by Kim Zetter, WIRED, 20 January 2005

The published interview is edited for length and organization. Sanborn connects his earlier natural-force artworks to his selection for the CIA commission. He explains the transition from revealing nature's hidden phenomena to addressing human secrecy, and presents lodestones as continuity with his previous practice. [S06]

Crucially, when asked whether other physical elements contain clues, he does not authenticate a particular interpretation. He also says physical access to CIA headquarters is not necessary to solve the text, while allowing that deciphered information could refer to something there. These statements permit a physical theme or eventual referent; they do not endorse a compass-based K4 recipe.

### Sanborn interviewed by Avis Berman, Smithsonian, 14–16 July 2009

The complete Archives of American Art transcript was retrieved, not just the search extract. Relevant passages in the 15 July first recording concern visualizing magnetic fields with compasses, earlier shadow installations, and collecting lodestones in Utah with scientific assistance from NASA's Peter Wasilewski. The field-visualization discussion is around 00:10–00:12; shadows around 00:50–00:52; lodestone collecting around 00:54–01:02. [S07]

This is a retrospective firsthand account of artistic practice. It does not securely trace the specific installed CIA rock from quarry to final placement. During the earlier-exhibition discussion, a 1982 postcard and his 1981 notes disagree, and he acknowledges the discrepancy. That supports preserving, rather than smoothing over, the Artists Space dating problem.

### Undated artist pitch letter in a later archival-photo gallery

A photograph on Kryptosbot's archive page visibly shows a letter describing Sanborn's geological materials, lightning-magnetized lodestones, and CIA water features. The page owner says the photographs were made during Smithsonian archival research in March 2026. The letter image itself was inspected. [S23, S25]

The image is useful supporting evidence but lacks a visible date and a verified box/folder citation. Its author's future-tense reference to completing the CIA work in spring cannot independently fix its year. It should be cited as an **undated letter reproduced in a third-party archival photograph**, not as a newly authenticated dated CIA proposal.

## 4. Magnetism: fact, representation, and later interpretation

### Supported at different evidentiary levels

- **Original design fact:** an entrance compass rose was to be inscribed and accompanied by lodestone. [S01]
- **Original artist explanation:** the entrance rock was naturally magnetized by lightning and evoked ancient navigation. This is Sanborn's account, not a modern mineralogical assay of the object. [S02]
- **Earlier installation mechanism:** movable needles were placed in a local magnetic field in the Artists Space installation. [S08]
- **Presentational metaphor:** Arlington's official description treats its engraved needle as being drawn toward the lodestone, giving the image an unreliable-direction theme. [S10]
- **Explicit Kryptos textual connection:** the accepted K2 plaintext includes “THEY USED THE EARTHS MAGNETIC FIELD.” This establishes a magnetic-field motif inside the encrypted text; it does not identify an operation to apply to K4. [S19]

### Not established by the sources inspected

- A measured CIA lodestone field strength, polarity, field map, or distance-dependent deflection curve.
- A documented rule that passing compasses must point west-southwest, regardless of the observer's position.
- A mechanically moving needle installed on the entrance stone.
- An artist-authenticated connection from a particular physical bearing to K4 extraction, rotation, projection, or substitution.
- A demonstrated requirement to use a specific year's magnetic declination.

The historical intent of disorienting or revealing magnetic forces can be real without making every later directional calculation artist-authorized. A claim about the **direction of a carved image**, a claim about a **rock's local magnetic field**, and a claim about a **cipher operation** require three different kinds of evidence.

### WIRED's 2009 feature and the NSA papers

Steven Levy's 20 April 2009 WIRED feature describes the entrance lodestone and the compass etched in granite. Its account of suspected clues in the compass, Morse text, and fountain is attributed to speculation, not to Sanborn confirming a procedure. Its anecdote about a missing truckload of stone is not evidence that the installed lodestone was later replaced. [S17]

NSA's released document 3 contains a December 1991 study-group context and contemporary analysis of the sculpture. It identifies a carved compass and magnetic stone, but the directional descriptions and handwritten correction belong to an analyst's working material. Declassification does not convert those annotations into artist instructions. The file also contains tentative cryptanalytic assumptions, underscoring that it is evidence of investigation rather than a design specification. [S18]

## 5. Shadows, astronomy, and astrology

**Shadows:** Sanborn's own portfolio lists Thunderhead Horizon, 1985, using sandstone, lodestone, and shadow. That independently confirms shadow as a material of his earlier practice. It does not show that the Kryptos compass is a sundial or that solar alignment is a decoding instruction. [S09]

**Navigation:** this is the strongest direct association for the entrance lodestone because it occurs in the signed 1989 explanatory letter. Navigation should not be silently expanded into a specific celestial-navigation technique. [S02]

**Astrology:** Bethesda Urban Partnership explicitly applies that term to **Hidden Under the Three Events**, alongside physical science and archaeology. It is institutional interpretation of a separate work, not a recovered Sanborn statement about Kryptos. WIRED's 2009 article also discusses astrology among solver approaches. Neither source supports importing an astrological system into K4. [S13, S17]

## 6. Materials, dimensions, alterations, and remaining gaps

### Materials and dimensions

- The CIA's 1989 bulletin securely gives the commission's material vocabulary, but not a chemical identification or measurements for the lodestone alone. [S01]
- GSA's overall dimensions are listed as varying. [S05]
- The artist's website supplies **12 × 20 × 10 feet** for its Kryptos courtyard-plaza entry. These are not the dimensions of the entrance compass or rock. [S09]
- Dimensions reported for Arlington, Bethesda, the courtyard screen, and earlier gallery installations must remain attached to those specific objects. No reliable original dimensioned entrance drawing was recovered in this portion of the research.

### Replacement and relocation

No retrieved commission document, institutional account, or firsthand interview establishes that the installed CIA lodestone or compass rose was replaced, remagnetized, or relocated after installation. This is a **gap in the reviewed evidence**, not proof that no conservation work occurred. Construction-stage movement of stone and the missing-truckload anecdote should not be rewritten as post-installation conservation history.

### Exact unresolved questions

1. **As-built specifications:** original plan, stone schedule, compass carving drawing, and dimensions of the entrance elements.
2. **Magnetic characterization:** mineral composition, remanent magnetization, polarity, and documented field measurements of the actual CIA stone.
3. **Construction and conservation records:** dated placement photographs, maintenance reports, replacement orders, and relocation history.
4. **Dedication date:** reconcile 3 November on the current CIA page with 5 November on the released remarks and program caption.
5. **Earlier-work chronology:** reconcile Artists Space's 1982 Invisible Forces record with the artist's 1981 North by Northwest listing; establish Find the Lodestone's installation date and dimensions from primary project records.
6. **Archival provenance:** identify box/folder/item citations for the undated pitch letter and compass sketches reproduced by Kryptosbot. The Smithsonian finding aid identifies relevant collections, but item images were not fully accessible through the retrieved folder pages. [S16, S23]

No contact with CIA, GSA, Smithsonian staff, the artist, or private collectors was made. No hypothesis testing was performed.

## 7. Method and source register

Exa was used for discovery and full-page retrieval; Firecrawl was used for full-page and PDF retrieval, including the complete Smithsonian oral history. Search variants included both **lodestone** and **loadstone**. The research used **8 Exa search requests, requesting 75 result slots in total**. Those are requested discovery slots, not 75 unique reviewed sources.

The register below contains **25 distinct, substantively inspected source URLs**, including an individually inspected image. It is a count of retained evidence items, not all search hits or every contextual URL opened. Reprints and third-party mirrors are labeled and are not treated as independent confirmation merely because they have different URLs. Original facsimiles of the 1989 bulletin, both pages of the signed letter, and the undated pitch-letter photograph were visually checked. Search snippets alone were not used to establish a substantive claim.

The Washington Post's 14 January 1990 article was located but its full text was not recovered; it is not used as full-text evidence here. Paradigm's history page likewise did not yield substantive page content, so the separate archive gallery, rather than the history narrative, is the source retained below. No full copyrighted source text is reproduced in this dossier.

## Bottom line

The lodestone and rose are original, intentional parts of Kryptos's entrance sequence. The best contemporaneous explanation is ancient navigation within a broader theme of hidden natural information. Sanborn's magnetic and shadow artworks provide real historical context. They do not, without further evidence, turn a carved bearing, a nearby magnetic effect, or a reference in another sculpture into an authenticated K4 instruction.
