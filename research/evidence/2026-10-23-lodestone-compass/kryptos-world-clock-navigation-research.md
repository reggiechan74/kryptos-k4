# Alexanderplatz World Clock: navigation, astronomical symbolism and historical-state evidence

Research date: 23 October 2026. Evidence collection only. No cipher, solar, date, bearing-fit or plaintext hypothesis was tested. No third party was contacted.

## Executive findings

1. The World Clock connection to K4 is now an artist-confirmed referent, not merely a solver's guess. Sanborn's 12 November 2025 open letter identifies the Berlin World Clock and associates it with the crowds involved in the fall of the Wall. This does **not** establish a cipher formula, a compass transformation, a particular date/time, a navigation instruction in unknown plaintext, or that the clock cannot be an arithmetic component. [S1–S2]
2. The clock and Kryptos really do have compass-rose elements, but recognizing that correspondence is not new. A public post dated 23 November 2014 explicitly connected the Urania World Clock's compass-rose base to Kryptos's compass rose and lodestone. Its November/December 2014 comments make the choice of Urania unmistakable. This is prior art, not proof that the proposed connection solves anything. [S3]
3. The clock is an electrically driven world-time display with a moving 1–24 hour ring. Its upper planetary structure is symbolic and rotates as a whole once per minute. The maker's mechanical description was already in the existing research. A newly located direct interview adds much stronger design-intent evidence: Erich John says realistic planetary periods were considered but not implemented because of the gearing problem. [S4–S5]
4. A wind/compass rose exists, but no north-referenced as-built plan, surveyed mounting angle or authoritative fixed panel-to-true-north registration was located. None of the reviewed descriptions justifies claiming a particular panel lies on an exact ENE/WSW axis. The rotating numeral ring is not a fixed geographic origin. [S6–S8]
5. Modern city lists are not historical inventories. The clock was designed for updates; John describes replaceable plates and an intended ten-year maintenance/update cycle. The December 1997 restoration added 20 names and changed some assignments. Today's reported counts themselves disagree. Complete 1989 plate coverage and global registration remain missing. [S5, S9–S11]
6. There are now concrete archival leads rather than only generic suggestions: John says the original timezone study was commissioned from the Geodetic Institute in Potsdam, and Stefan Kunsch reported in 2024 that his firm still has the necessary construction documents and original lettering templates. Neither archive was accessed in this pass. [S5, S12]

## 1. What Sanborn actually confirmed

### Direct letter and independent reporting

The DocumentCloud document is titled *Kryptos Clues – Nov. 12, 2025* and described as an open letter from Jim Sanborn. Exa supplied its extracted text after Firecrawl failed to retrieve the document. The facsimile itself was not visually inspected in this pass, so unusual wording should be checked against the image before verbatim quotation. [S1]

The extracted letter identifies:

- Sanborn's **second Egypt trip in late 1986**, without an exact day or month.
- The fall of the Berlin Wall as another relevant event.
- The Berlin World Clock as the clock intended by the K4 reference, described in terms of its role as a gathering place.
- The overall codes, from Morse through K5, as concerned with delivering a message.

Dan Vergano's 12 November 2025 Scientific American report independently reports those core clues from the International Spy Museum appearance and links the letter. Its introduction incorrectly calls K4 79 letters before later using 97; that typo is not evidence for an alternate ciphertext length. The letter's extracted wording places plaintext writing in 1988 while mentioning the 1989 Wall event. Preserve this chronology wrinkle rather than inventing a correction or deriving a date from it. [S1–S2]

**Permitted conclusion:** the World Clock is a documented referent and late 1986 is a bounded historical clue.

**Not established:** any exact Egypt date, celestial configuration, setting of the hour ring, direction of traversal, special compass bearing, particular cryptographic formula, or the content of unknown plaintext surrounding BERLINCLOCK. The clarification alone cannot prove that the clock is merely a waypoint or that it is not part of an arithmetic mechanism.

## 2. Prior public connection: the 2014 compass-rose proposal

José Galofré Manero's post, under joseph4barna/josephfromspain, bears the title *KRYPTOS K4 (part 4): BERLIN CLOCK, KRYPTOS & A COMPASS ROSE*. The page date is 23 November 2014; returned publication metadata is 2014-11-23T00:24:08Z and modification metadata 2014-12-23T22:52:22Z. [S3]

The author explicitly compares the CIA installation's compass rose/lodestone with the Urania World Clock at Alexanderplatz, links photographic evidence, and wonders whether this is coincidence. A 25 November comment endorses Urania because both works have compass roses and interpret time/space. A 17 December comment again selects Urania and speculates about circular cipher devices. These are actual World-Clock/compass proposals, not incidental mentions of the same keywords.

This contradicts a literal assertion that **all** researchers focused exclusively on the Mengenlehreuhr for eleven years. It does not quantify what most researchers studied. The page's dates and contemporaneously dated comments are useful provenance, but a separately timestamped archival capture was not obtained; site metadata alone is not tamper-proof historical certification.

The author's astronomical labels and cipher suggestions are not adopted as facts. In particular, calling the World Clock an atomic clock in a comment does not override its documented electric/mechanical construction. A proposal made before a later clue is still a proposal, not an independently validated K4 mechanism.

## 3. Physical construction and actual motion

Kunsch Metallbau's project description is a maker/restorer source, rather than a solver interpretation. It describes a roughly 10 m, 16 t structure with a 5.5 m diameter drum, upper and lower rings of 24 aluminum plates, a middle ring numbered 1–24 making one revolution per day, and a simplified planetary structure turning once per minute. The drive was developed at VEB Optische Werke Rathenow. [S4]

Berlin's official visitor description independently distinguishes the rotating hour ring from the city labels, describes the ground wind rose, and identifies a converted Trabant gearbox. John explains the moving 24-hour scale in a Deutschlandfunk interview; the outer labels represent the world while the scale moves through the time zones. [S6, S13]

### What is new about the solar-system evidence

In the 22 April 2022 nd interview, John explains that he initially wanted to represent the Earth's and other planets' actual orbital motions, then chose a symbolic representation because the required gearing was impractical. This is substantially stronger than merely noticing an ornamental globe in a photograph. The interview includes rough planetary-period figures that should not be reused as accurate astronomical data. Its relevant evidential value is the designer's account of what was **not built**. [S5]

Consequences:

- Do not read present ornament positions as an ephemeris or assume independent planets reproduce physical orbital periods.
- A one-minute global rotation is not a date encoding by itself.
- This does not rule out an as-yet-evidenced symbolic use of the ornament, or sunlight/shadow in a hypothesis. It does rule out treating accurate planetary motion as a documented feature.

### Sundial versus sunlight effect

None of the reviewed maker, designer or institutional sources describes an operating sundial or a calibrated astronomical observing instrument. The documented timekeeping is electric/mechanical. Bildhauerei in Berlin's object description records an interesting optical effect: sunlight passing through a city inscription can project lettering onto the gold decorative strip. That is genuine observational evidence for light interacting with the structure, but no calibrated shadow dial, equation of time, gnomon alignment or measurement procedure is supplied. Calling it a sundial would go beyond the sources. [S4, S7]

## 4. Compass rose, geographic orientation and reading conventions

The institutional accounts consistently describe a wind rose beneath the column. A 2022 accessibility description gives useful tactile detail: metal lines, letters and numbers inset into a circular granite surface, with lines running toward the column. This establishes physical markings, not survey accuracy. Its description of the numbers as longitude/latitude is not independently validated here. [S6, S8]

Bildhauerei in Berlin also records missing floor mosaics in an object-condition entry associated with its 2009 documentation. That and the current granite/inlay description warn against assuming every modern floor feature is unchanged from 1969 or 1989. They do **not** identify the date, extent or orientation of any replacement. [S7–S8]

No reviewed source supplies all of the following together:

1. A surveyed true-north line, explicitly distinguished from magnetic north or map-grid north.
2. A fixed named city panel tied to that line.
3. The installation/restoration date to which that registration applies.
4. A clear viewing convention for clockwise/counterclockwise rotation.

Therefore exact World Clock orientation remains unresolved. A modern 3D solver reconstruction appeared in searches but expressly states that drum-to-north alignment and geometry are approximate; it is not an independent survey and was not promoted as evidence. Nor can a single photograph's moving hour numerals provide a permanent panel origin.

Kunsch's numbered range is 1–24, not a physical label 0. Converting dial 24 to residue zero is a modeling convention, already stated as such in the existing project report. The current pass does not choose a new zero, direction, global phase or bearing. The ideal 16-point compass definitions of ENE and WSW are not evidence that either physical artwork is precisely aligned to those bearings.

## 5. Historical state, updates and counts

### Existing partial inventory remains partial

The reviewed repository integration at commit d47d8768 reports **41 labels, ten physical panels and seventeen panel/band cells in three disconnected dated photo segments**. The source dates include 1969, 1970 and 1984, not complete 1989 coverage. That is the existing baseline, not a new result of this research pass. Its successful round trips were implementation checks, not proof of historical completeness. No tests were rerun. [B1–B2]

John's 2022 interview explicitly supports intentional changeability: the plates were designed to be replaceable, and technical maintenance plus updating was intended every ten years. He also says he commissioned the Geodetic Institute Potsdam to study the selected cities' timezones before political approval. This undermines any assumption that a generic pre-restoration image necessarily fixes the 1989 state. [S5]

### 1997 restoration: contemporaneous evidence

The 12 December 1997 Berliner Zeitung report says Kunsch's team was reinstalling plates; twenty city names had been added, hour numerals regilded, and worn drive components replaced. Examples of additions are Oslo, Jerusalem and Cape Town. It specifically explains Kyiv's change from Moscow time after the Soviet Union's dissolution. This supports a real historical version boundary, not a complete before/after inventory. [S9]

Berlin.de's later description also mentions corrected timezone assignments and added cities. The existing project report already used this 1997 evidence and Kunsch's mechanism description. They are **revalidated here**, not newly discovered. [S6, B2]

### Conflicting counts must not become cryptographic parameters

- The World Clock organization says originally **80** city names and currently **146** city/region names, plus the date line. It is the commercial/heritage organization associated with the clock's promotion, not the Berlin government's monument database. [S10]
- A March 2019 B.Z. interview's photo caption says originally **97**, later **148**. This conflicts with the organization and does not include a plate-by-plate enumeration. [S11]
- Other search results repeat 148, including Wikipedia and a Rathenow tourist leaflet; repetition does not reconcile the counting definitions or date.

Do not silently select 97 because K4 has 97 characters, or 148 because a solver page uses that number. Counts may involve differing dates, definitions or errors; this pass did not resolve which. None of these numbers is an established 1989 inventory count. The known twenty 1997 additions cannot reconcile all differences by simple subtraction.

Kunsch's broad upper/lower hemisphere description also cannot overrule literal placement in historical photographs. The existing baseline observes Ankara and Cairo in a lower band, so the band is not automatically a strict north/south-hemisphere bit. [S4, B2]

## 6. New historical visual leads, not a completed inventory

### Early design drawing

SPIEGEL's 30 September 2019 interview/gallery reproduces John's initial calendar sketch, credited to Elisa Zadek/Weltzeituhr-Berlin.de. The visible pages say 24 and 25 June; the accompanying story places the sketches in 1968. It shows alternative column/drum/planet forms, not a north-referenced engineering plan. The printed page dates alone do not establish a year. The image was visually inspected. [S14]

### Feist historical photographs

Bildhauerei in Berlin provides images with filenames identifying Feist, including HU-088-07 labeled 1969 and HU-087-80/HU-108-31 labeled 1971. The 1969-labeled image was visually inspected and shows the American arc plus the original-looking planetary structure and floor. It is potentially useful for source-specific comparisons, but a filename is weaker than a complete catalog record, the full ring is absent, and no labels were added to the admitted dataset. [S7]

In particular, possible differences from the already admitted January 1970 New York panel placement must not be resolved by silently merging images. They require dated source verification and an independent seam-level reading. The prior report's specific New York placement remains its own observation; this pass neither overwrites it nor declares it erroneous.

### May 1989 photograph

SPIEGEL gallery item 11 is captioned as the May 1989 FDJ Whitsun gathering, photographed by Tobias Seel, snapshot-photography/ullstein bild. It shows people sheltering under part of the clock, including some Asia/Pacific lower panels. It was visually inspected. The upper portion is cut off, text is small, the view is oblique, and it is not complete circumferential coverage. It is a genuinely additional dated visual lead relative to the reviewed baseline, **not a complete 1989 list or global registration**. [S14]

Source images were retained for private research only. The dossier does not grant redistribution rights; each rights holder's terms apply.

## 7. Historical context and source disagreements

Kunsch supports the approximately 16 t and 10 m dimensions quoted in the user's supplied passage. The World Clock organization reports that opposition demonstrators assembled by the clock on 7 October 1989 and that the Wall fell 33 days later. DDR Museum historian Stefan Wolle independently discusses the 7 October demonstration and the clock as its meeting place. The political gathering is therefore well grounded; treating that context as a proven plaintext waypoint is not. [S4, S10, S15]

The inauguration date is not perfectly consistent across sources. Berlin.de, the clock organization and SPIEGEL give 30 September 1969. Deutschlandfunk gives 2 October, the DDR Museum describes motion beginning 3 October, and Bildhauerei in Berlin gives 7 October. These may concern different milestones or include errors. The Berlin heritage database dates the monument 1969–1970. This pass uses 30 September only when attributed, not as a silently unanimous date. [S6–S7, S10, S13–S16]

The heritage database's record is object 09020854, classified as a listed sculpture/clock, with John, Kunsch and Müller credited. It lists relevant historical literature: Hans-Werner Klünner's *Straßenmöbel in Berlin* (1983), a Joachim Näther interview in *BZ am Abend* (17 April 1968), and Danuta Schmidt's 2013 article. It does not expose a construction plan or complete plate inventory. [S16]

## 8. Concrete next evidence targets and stopping conditions

These are research targets, not actions already taken or requests sent:

1. **Kunsch construction archive.** B.Z.'s 23 November 2024 workshop report quotes Stefan Kunsch saying the firm retained necessary construction records and found John's 1969 letter templates. The strongest requests would be the fixed city-ring layout by revision, north-referenced installation plan, and 1997 before/after plate schedule. The report proves records were claimed to exist, not that they contain every needed field or are publicly available. [S12]
2. **Original timezone study.** John's reference to the Geodetic Institute Potsdam provides an institutional provenance lead. The study, title, accession number, date and present custodian were not located. Do not equate the institute with a present organization without verifying archive succession. [S5]
3. **Berlin conservation/municipal files.** Record 09020854 and Bildhauerei's bibliography point toward the 1993 Köcher catalog and 2003 Trost monument topography. Those works and conservation dossiers were not obtained. They may contain floor or plate information, but that is only a lead. [S7, S16]
4. **Dated photographic circumferential reconstruction.** Obtain readable overlapping 1989 views or documented continuity for each consumed cell. Keep distinct historical states, city names, line wraps, panel seams, bands and source dates. A 1969/1970/1984 collage must never be relabeled 1989.
5. **Orientation evidence.** Obtain a surveyed/as-built relation between true north, floor markings and a named fixed panel. Record tolerances and historical date. A magnetic compass next to large steelwork, a generic geotag or model alignment is not automatically sufficient.

A proposed physical reconstruction remains unverified if it requires an unobserved panel, an unsupported alphabet-to-city mapping, an unmeasured bearing or an invented Egypt date. A declared bounded search over keys or phases can be explored conditionally, but cannot turn its fitted assumptions into historical observations. The present dossier leaves these gaps explicit and supplies no K4 solution claim.

## Final novelty ledger

**New relative to the two reviewed baseline reports:** explicit 2014 compass-rose prior art; John's 2022 rejection of literal planetary gearing; his Geodetic Institute study and intentional update history; Kunsch's 2024 surviving-records statement; extra historical photo/sketch leads; direct comparison of conflicting counts; floor-condition caution; explicit limits on what the 2025 referent clue entails.

**Revalidated, not new:** general compass-rose presence; 24-panel structure; daily numbered-ring movement; one-minute planetary ornament; 1997 additions and Kyiv change; incomplete 1989 coverage and absent fixed registration.

**Still not established:** complete 1989 inventory, historical north alignment, exact ring direction/zero convention, a date-specific planetary state, functioning sundial, exact Egypt trip date, connection from either compass rose to a validated K4 formula or plaintext instruction.
