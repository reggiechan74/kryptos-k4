# Kryptos lodestone and compass evidence

Research date: 23 October 2026

This report examines the physical installation, original artist explanations, the history of directional claims, and the Alexanderplatz World Clock connection. It prepares the evidence needed to evaluate three proposed mechanisms without running them. No cipher search, bearing fit, solar calculation, celestial reconstruction, or new plaintext test was performed. No exact K4 mechanism is established.

## Executive summary

- **The rose and lodestone are at the west entrance to the New Headquarters Building, not beside the copper screen.** The screen occupies the northwest courtyard. They are separate parts of the same commission, with the building between the entrance approach and courtyard. Public evidence does not supply a surveyed compass-to-screen bearing or distance. [CIA] [BULLETIN89] [AERIAL]
- **The carved needle and a real compass are different objects.** Photographs show an engraved compass rose, a distinct needle-like figure, and a separate nearby stone outside the circle. Two accounts of a 2005 meeting report that the needle was intended to point toward the lodestone. Neither account measures its installed geographical bearing. [GILLOGLY] [DW05] [CH05]
- **Ancient navigation is an original, documented association.** Sanborn's signed 15 December 1989 explanation identifies the entrance lodestone and its navigational meaning. The inspected document is a cleaned, third-party-hosted facsimile of released CIA record C00042168, not a newly obtained archival original. [LETTER89]
- **WSW is opposite ENE, but exact WSW has not been measured here.** Standard compass names give ENE as 67.5° and WSW as 247.5°. The various published SW, SSW, 220°, 230°–240° and 247.5° statements concern different axes and have different provenance. They cannot be averaged, equated, or converted into one precise installed bearing.
- **The World Clock connection is real, but its mechanics remain unspecified.** Sanborn's 2025 letter identifies the World Clock; a public 2014 proposal had already connected its compass rose with Kryptos's rose and lodestone. The clock's upper planetary structure is deliberately symbolic, not a working ephemeris. Complete 1989 city labels and a historical true-north registration remain missing. [LETTER25] [COMPASS14] [JOHN22]
- **All three hypotheses still need independent inputs.** Bearing-to-clock mapping lacks a verified physical reference frame and mapping rule. Shadow-based reading lacks calibrated geometry and an independently chosen observation time. A dated celestial reading additionally lacks a specific date, target and demonstrated observing mechanism. Artistic relevance is not a decoding instruction.

## 1 Where the entrance components sit and what they face

### Location relative to the copper screen

The 17 May 1989 CIA employee bulletin separates the commission into an entrance installation and a courtyard installation. It places the first at the west entrance to the New Headquarters Building, along the walkway from the parking deck, and the main screen and petrified tree in the northwest courtyard. The current CIA description preserves that distinction. These sources support a stronger answer than loosely calling all the objects part of the courtyard. [BULLETIN89] [CIA]

Elonka Dunin's published whole-site aerial supplies the broad spatial context: the New Headquarters Building separates the entrance approach from the courtyard. The entrance rose and courtyard screen are not neighboring objects facing one another across a small open space. The aerial page itself acknowledges uncertainty about the exact entrance placement. Its image is useful for location, not a substitute for a north-referenced survey. [AERIAL]

Accordingly, the defensible description is **west entrance rose and lodestone; northwest courtyard screen; intervening building**. No exact compass-to-screen azimuth, separation, unobstructed sightline, or intended long-distance pointing relationship is established by the reviewed material.

### What is engraved and what is magnetic

The CIA photograph and Jim Gillogly's 1999 photographs show a rose inscribed in a flat slab, a long double-ended needle-like carving, and a separate stone beyond the engraved circle. The lodestone is not at the rose's center. The needle-like carving does not align with the rose's engraved N–S axis. These are visual observations, not measurements of true north. [CIA] [GILLOGLY]

Five quantities must remain separate:

1. **Engraved N axis:** the fixed line from the rose center toward its N marking.
2. **Carved needle axis:** the long depicted needle, with two opposite ends. Choosing one end as the north-seeking tip requires evidence.
3. **Rose-to-stone direction:** a vector between defined points on the rose and stone. The stone's geometric center need not be a magnetic pole.
4. **True north:** an external geographical reference, distinct from grid north and magnetic north.
5. **A real compass reading:** the response of a movable magnetized needle at a stated position, height and time near the artwork.

A lodestone can affect a separate magnetic compass brought nearby. It cannot rotate an engraved design. A photograph of the carving does not document the behavior of a visitor's compass, and a qualitative magnetic description does not specify a constant reading at every nearby position.

### The best evidence for intended pointing

Two attendee accounts of the 29 October 2005 Sanborn lunch agree that the carved needle was intended to point toward the lodestone. Dunin and Wilson's raw notes also report that the rose was intended to point north, with a tentative magnetic-variation qualification. Hanson's notes do not independently measure either direction. [DW05] [CH05] [EVENT05]

These are recollections, not a signed construction drawing or verbatim recorded transcript. They also differ on Sanborn's response to additional supposed targets: one account describes him as unaware of them, the other as declining to answer. The accounts support local needle-to-stone intent; they do not justify a categorical denial or confirmation of every further alignment.

An indexed CIA Intellipedia record from 2014 also distinguishes the engraved needle from magnetic north and describes it as pointing at the lodestone. That is useful corroborating description, but it is a compilation with secondary references, not an independent field log. Its live record redirected during retrieval; the recovered evidence is Exa-indexed text. [INTELLIPEDIA]

## 2 Why the published directions do not agree

ENE and WSW are diametrically opposite in the standard 16-point system. That answers the geometrical question directly. It does not establish that Kryptos's carved needle has a surveyed bearing of 247.5°, or that ENE is the opposite direction to use in a cipher.

For comparison, SW is 225°, SSW is 202.5°, and WSW is 247.5°. A report of about 220° is close to SW, not an exact statement of SSW or WSW. A line can also be described by either of its opposite directions, whereas an arrow or north-seeking tip requires an identified endpoint.

### The 1991 document must be read as an investigation

NSA document 3 is DOCID 4112086, released in 2014. Its second physical PDF page contains an 18 November 1991 memorandum; the compass discussion is on physical PDF page 4, internally numbered page 3. The compilation also includes December 1991 study-group material. It is not a Sanborn-authored specification. [NSA91]

Visual inspection of page 4 supports reading the marginal numbers as approximately 230°–240°, but the preceding handwritten symbol is unclear. More importantly, the annotation does not identify a surveying method or securely assign the number to engraved N, the carved needle, or an actual magnetic reading. OCR displaced the handwriting into a heading, so text extraction alone is insufficient. The uncertainty belongs in the evidence record; it must not become a precise parameter.

The memo's other tentative cryptanalytic assumptions show what investigators were considering at the time. Its government provenance establishes authenticity as an investigation, not correctness as an interpretation of the artwork.

### How later precision entered the discussion

Monet Friedrich's reproduced diagrams, Lann's photographic overlays, and Numberworld's posts are useful records of previous analytical approaches. They do not supply a calibrated camera model, surveyed north line, instrument log, or uncertainty budget. The original SFU analysis page was unavailable, while a reproduction survives. Repetition downstream cannot repair those missing inputs. [MORSE] [LANNCOMPASS] [LANNLINES] [NUMBER20] [NUMBER22]

The current SolveKryptos description incorrectly places the rose near the screen in the courtyard and the stone at its center. Its field guide then uses exact directional values in a proposed solution. Treating those solution inputs as independently measured physical facts would make the argument circular. The Academia paper landing page inspected in this research exposed an abstract rather than the full physical-evidence section; its directional claims were not admitted as verified measurements. ## 3 The original 1989 explanation

### The employee bulletin

Released CIA record C00042166 is the 17 May 1989 employee bulletin describing Sanborn's approved proposal. The inspected facsimile visibly carries the date, CIA emblem, release marking and later image-cleaning watermark. It describes the compass rose and lodestone before completion of the commission. Its declared subject is the accumulation of information through natural processes and written forms. [BULLETIN89]

This is contemporary evidence that the entrance components were intentional from the design stage. It does not specify a bearing, the stone's measured magnetic properties, a hidden second magnet, or an operation for K4.

### Sanborn's signed letter

Released record C00042168 contains Sanborn's signed explanation to agency employees, dated 15 December 1989. Both pages were visually checked. The date, release markings, signature and CIA/Public Affairs fax header are visible. Sanborn describes the entrance rock as naturally magnetized by lightning and links it to ancient navigation. He also explains layered stone, hidden geological information, the progression of codes, water and petrified wood. [LETTER89]

The project was still about two-thirds complete when he wrote. The letter therefore provides direct design intent, not a completed as-built survey. It supplies no precise direction, observation date, astronomical target, astrological system, or magnetic-declination instruction.

**Provenance limit:** the copies retrieved are hosted by The Kryptos Project and marked as cleaned by JEW-LEE. They are facsimiles of released records, not untouched originals independently downloaded from CIA. The November–December 1991 issue of The Cryptogram independently establishes early circulation of the dated explanation, but reprinting the same letter does not create a second independent statement of intent. [CRYPTOGRAM91]

The current CIA page, GSA catalog and artist portfolio disagree in some dating details. CIA gives 3 November 1990 for dedication, while released Webster remarks and a reproduced dedication-program caption give 5 November. The artist's portfolio labels Kryptos 1989, and GSA dates it 1990. This report uses the construction period 1989–1990 rather than resolving the discrepancy by assumption. [CIA] [GSA] [WEBSTER90] [PORTFOLIO] [PARADIGMARCHIVE]

## 5 What the text and opposite faces establish

### K1 and K2 provide themes rather than a K4 procedure

K1's accepted plaintext concerns light, shadow and visual illusion. K2 explicitly invokes the Earth's magnetic field, underground information and a geographical coordinate. Those themes make investigation of optics and magnetism reasonable; they do not say how to transform K4. The NSA summary provides the accepted-text record used for this distinction. Literal transcriptions should preserve the intentional spellings IQLUSION and UNDERGRUUND rather than silently correcting them. [NSASUMMARY]

Three separate propositions must not be collapsed: the artwork contains magnetic and optical themes; those themes could relate to K4; a particular magnetic or optical operation decodes K4. The first is documented. The latter two require further evidence and, eventually, properly constrained tests.

Sanborn's 12 November 2025 letter identifies the Berlin World Clock and discusses his second Egypt trip in late 1986 and the fall of the Berlin Wall. Scientific American independently reports the event and links the letter. The letter was retrieved as extracted text through Exa after a Firecrawl failure; its facsimile was not visually inspected in that part of the research. A chronology wrinkle involving writing in 1988 and the 1989 Wall event is preserved rather than repaired by invention. [LETTER25] [SA25]

These clues do not choose an exact Egypt date, solar position, star, clock setting, geographical bearing, or numerical mapping. Nor do they prove that the World Clock is only a waypoint and cannot contribute arithmetic. The interpretation remains open within the documented constraints.

### The tableau really is oriented for the opposite side

The current CIA description explicitly distinguishes the ciphertext half from the alphabet-table half when viewed from the front, and says the table was deliberately flipped to be read from the back. The letters are cut through the copper rather than simply printed on one surface. Thus the observation that the two halves have different readable faces has documentary support. [CIA]

That does **not** automatically reverse the K4 character sequence or turn an ENE bearing into WSW. Mirror-reading a cutout glyph, reversing character order, viewing the opposite face, and reversing a geographical vector are different transformations. Any later mechanism must specify which transformation it proposes and why. A rendered model must first reproduce the actual face arrangement before it can support such reasoning.

## 6 What the Alexanderplatz World Clock contributes

### A confirmed referent with an older compass proposal

Sanborn's 2025 clarification makes the World Clock an artist-confirmed referent. However, the compass connection itself is not a new global discovery. José Galofré Manero's post dated 23 November 2014 explicitly compares Kryptos's compass rose and lodestone with the Urania World Clock at Alexanderplatz. Dated November and December comments make the intended clock unambiguous. [LETTER25] [COMPASS14]

No separately timestamped archival capture was obtained, so the page's date and comments should be described as website evidence rather than tamper-proof certification. They are nevertheless clear prior public proposals. Claims that every researcher focused exclusively on the Mengenlehreuhr until 2025 are too strong. Prior publication also does not validate the proposed connection as a solution.

### Documented mechanics

Kunsch Metallbau's maker/restorer account describes a drum with upper and lower rings of 24 plates and a middle scale numbered 1–24 rotating once per day. The upper planetary structure rotates as a whole once per minute. Berlin's official description independently distinguishes city labels, the rotating hour scale and the ground wind rose. [KUNSCH] [BERLIN]

Erich John gave particularly useful design-intent evidence in a 2022 interview: realistic orbital motions were considered but not implemented because of the gearing problem. The built planets are symbolic. Their visible positions therefore cannot be treated as an astronomical ephemeris. [JOHN22]

No reviewed maker, designer or institutional description establishes a calibrated sundial. Bildhauerei in Berlin records sunlight projecting city lettering onto a decorative strip. That is an optical effect, not a documented gnomon, calibrated shadow scale or observing procedure. [BILDHAUEREI]

The physical 24-position display does not by itself establish a 24-letter key, alphabet, traversal direction, zero convention or clock-to-cipher mapping. Treating the numeral 24 as modular zero is a model convention, not a missing inscription on the object.

### Historical labels and registration remain incomplete

John describes replaceable plates and an intended maintenance/update cycle. A contemporary December 1997 report documents twenty added city names and changed time assignments, including Kyiv's departure from Moscow time. A modern list cannot simply stand in for the 1989 clock. [JOHN22] [RESTORE97]

Counts also disagree. The World Clock organization reports an original 80 and a current 146 city/region names, plus the date line. A B.Z. interview's editorial photo caption reports an original 97 and later 148. These are not reconciled plate-by-plate inventories. Differences may reflect dates, counting definitions or errors; no evidence here decides which. Neither 146 nor 148 is an established 1989 count, and 97 must not be selected merely because K4 has 97 characters. [CLOCKORG] [BZ19]

The repository's prior historical-clock work admitted **41 labels, ten physical panels and seventeen panel/band cells**, distributed across three disconnected dated photo segments. Those are three different schema counts. They are not interchangeable with city totals, hour divisions, alphabet sizes or full circumference coverage. The photos span 1969, 1970 and 1984, not a complete 1989 inventory. No previous tests were rerun during this research. [BASEINTEGRATION] [BASECLOCK]

A newly inspected May 1989 gallery photograph provides an additional dated view of part of the Asia/Pacific region. Other newly inspected material includes John's early sketch and a Feist photograph labeled 1969. None completes the ring or establishes global registration. Potential differences from previously admitted observations must be resolved with source dates and seams, not silently merged. [SPIEGEL] [BILDHAUEREI]

No source reviewed here ties all four necessary facts together: true north, a named fixed city panel, a dated installation state, and an explicit viewing convention. The moving numeral ring cannot supply a permanent origin. A floor compass rose does not by itself prove survey accuracy. Condition documentation and differing floor descriptions also warn against assuming every current floor feature is unchanged since 1969 or 1989. [BERLIN] [BILDHAUEREI] [ACCESSIBILITY]

### The strongest next archival leads

John identifies the Geodetic Institute Potsdam as the source of the original city-timezone study. A 2024 workshop report quotes Stefan Kunsch saying construction records and original lettering templates survive. Berlin's heritage record supplies monument identifier 09020854 and historical bibliography. These are specific archival targets, but the relevant original plans and schedules were not obtained and no request was sent. [JOHN22] [BZ24] [HERITAGE]

## 8 Evidence readiness for the three hypotheses

| Hypothesis | Independent support available | Missing independent inputs | Readiness judgment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bearing or clock key | Original navigation theme; K4 directional/clock clues; two physical compass roses; clock's 24-position display | Surveyed axes, historical panel registration, endpoint choice, direction/phase and independently justified mapping | Relevant evidence exists, but no fixed-input mechanism is ready |
| Shadow orientation or time | K1 theme; actual light-letter observations; Sanborn's shadow practice; clock lettering projection | Calibrated geometry, face orientation, unobstructed versus reflected light, dated observation time, independently selected receiver and extraction rule | Physically motivated, but underconstrained |
| Dated celestial observation | Navigation association; late-1986 trip and 1989 historical clues; symbolic planetary imagery | Specific date/time/location, celestial target, observing method, historical orientation and extraction rule | No documented date-specific observing mechanism; literal planetary motion premise contradicted by designer |

This is an evidence-readiness assessment, not a probability ranking or a test result. A hypothesis can remain possible while being insufficiently specified for a faithful physical reconstruction. Declared, bounded exploratory searches can still examine conditional models; they must not be presented as measurements or historical verification.

### A bounded evidence gate before later tests

For any proposed operation, record the following before searching for successful outputs:

1. **Physical reference frame.** Identify the precise object, point, endpoint and axis; distinguish true, grid and magnetic north. Attach a date, measurement method and uncertainty.
2. **Historical state.** Identify which version of Kryptos or the World Clock the operation consumes. Do not mix unrelated dates, reconstructed plates or movable scale positions.
3. **Observation conditions.** For optics or magnetism, specify position, height, time zone, direct/reflected illumination or instrument conditions. Do not infer them from an attractive output.
4. **Transformation.** State separately any mirror reflection, sequence reversal, rotation, modular conversion, panel traversal and text extraction. Each needs independent justification.
5. **Declared search space and claim boundary.** A bounded unknown-key or phase search is legitimate if its range, model and acceptance criteria are declared in advance. Conditional exploration may also disclose hypothetical physical inputs. Such results cannot establish the actual historical geometry, missing city panels, observation date or measured bearing. Stop a claim of faithful physical reconstruction when those required facts remain unavailable; do not change the physical model after fitting and present the fitted assumptions as observations.

The most useful next evidence would be a north-referenced as-built entrance drawing, historical clock panel/plate schedules, and archival item-level provenance for the compass studies. These targets address missing inputs directly. Another speculative numerical fit would not.

### Retrieval method and counting

The research used both requested systems: Exa for discovery and indexed/full-page recovery, and Firecrawl for search and page/PDF extraction. Scanned and photographic evidence was visually inspected where identified, rather than relying solely on OCR. The three accompanying dossiers record source-level limitations.

The artist-history investigation used eight Exa searches requesting 75 result slots. The physical-evidence investigation used three Exa searches requesting 25 slots and eleven Firecrawl searches. The World Clock investigation used fourteen Exa searches requesting 107 slots, nine Firecrawl searches, and sixteen dedicated scrape attempts, fifteen of which produced readable pages. Thus the recorded Exa discovery total is **25 searches and 207 requested result slots**, not 207 sources read. Slots include repeats, irrelevant results and incomplete retrievals.

The retained source registers together contain **60 distinct textual web records, pages or PDFs, plus one separately registered archival photograph**, after deduplicating three URLs shared between the artist and physical dossiers. Additional visual images were used for verification without counting each image as an independent textual source. Two existing repository reports are listed separately as baseline material. This is a count of retained evidence items, not sixty independent primary attestations. The Cryptogram reprint, cleaned facsimiles, indexed copies and derivative solver accounts retain their dependency labels.

The scope is bounded public-source research. Some pages were cached, some live URLs failed or redirected, and some records were only partly accessible. In particular, the 2025 letter and two historical physical records were recovered as extracted/indexed text; the Academia landing page did not provide the full paper. Absence from this reviewed set is not proof that no measurement or archival plan exists elsewhere.

### What is new to this project and what is not

**Newly recovered or clarified in this pass:** the visually checked 1989 employee bulletin and signed letter; the differentiated history of Sanborn's magnetic installations; the source chain and contradictions behind directional claims; the 2014 World Clock compass proposal; John's account of rejected literal planetary gearing and planned plate updates; specific surviving-records leads; and additional dated historical images.

**Revalidated from existing research:** the World Clock's 24-position construction, daily scale and one-minute ornament motion; the 1997 changes; the prior partial historical inventory and absent global registration; and the distinction between physical artwork and an unproven K4 mechanism.

**Not new discoveries in the wider literature:** the existence of the lodestone/rose, the possibility of optical clues, 3D modeling, compass comparisons, and World Clock proposals. Firsthand 2002 light observations, 2003 measurement requests, 2005 modeling discussions and 2006 reporting already document a substantial history. [DUNININDEX] [WISHLIST] [DW05] [CH05] [WIRED06]
