# Kryptos projection photograph acquisition review

**Provenance:** authored research-worker report supplied verbatim as text by the parent on 24 October 2026. Inspection/search statements below describe that worker's work. Companion files mentioned below were not supplied; see the consumer [README](README.md). This is not an independent Mac source verification.

Research date: 24 October 2026. Purpose: find actual courtyard photographs that can support aperture-to-ground optical calibration, without manufacturing correspondences or treating duplicate image sizes as separate observations.

## Result

**The physical calibration gate remains open: zero certified aperture-to-ground pairs were established.** The review confirms additional, genuinely different photographic views containing visible transmitted-light patterns, but none yet supplies a uniquely assigned, spatially distributed set of pairs, measured scale, surveyed orientation, or authenticated exposure time.

The current best website illustration is the already-known Library of Congress Highsmith photograph, whose catalogue says there are no known publication restrictions. It should be shown as an observed pattern, not as calibrated evidence. The Smithsonian photograph folder remains the strongest acquisition lead; the exact reproduction process and current published rates are now verified.

## Annotations and the website distinction

The accompanying JSON records three manually inspected regions of interest in original-file coordinates, using origin at top left and rectangles [left, top, right, bottom):

- LOC ground detail: [1400, 2800, 2200, 3600] in the 3384 × 4283 original.
- CIA photo 5 ground detail: [100, 650, 620, 1100] in the 980 × 1305 JPEG.
- Gillogly tableau-overview ground detail: [45, 946, 155, 1024] in the 602 × 1024 JPEG.

These are **observation regions, not point correspondences**. No ground feature has been assigned a certified transmitting row/column. Confidence is high that the regions contain visible bright patterns; confidence in a particular aperture-to-ground identity is not established. The machine-readable certified-pair list is intentionally empty. An attractive V, X, Y or bar cannot identify a particular aperture when those forms recur across the tableau.

For the Site, keep two explicit modes:

1. **Synthetic physics demonstration.** Use deliberately chosen virtual aperture positions, screen geometry, receiver and source. Explain ray direction, blur, thickness and held-out prediction. Display synthetic inputs and results as synthetic. Synthetic correspondences may test implementation, but cannot certify the installed sculpture.
2. **Photographic evidence.** Show the LOC image and a movable/zoomable ground ROI. Display the zero-pair status and missing metadata. Do not overlay a fitted ray or a named aperture-ground match unless it has actually been established. Do not report invented residuals, camera values, dates or solved text.

A suitable caption is: “Observed light pattern, not a calibrated model. Exposure date and camera calibration unknown; no aperture-to-ground pairs certified.” A review-sized LOC derivative is supplied with dimensions, original hash and resize provenance in the JSON. The resize does not improve source resolution or add information.

## Access and reuse findings

The [LOC record](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/2011631531/) provides an explicit no-known-restrictions advisory and identifies the photographer as Carol M. Highsmith. Use its requested credit: “Photographs in the Carol M. Highsmith Archive, Library of Congress, Prints and Photographs Division.” The catalogue also warns that the digital representation can differ from the film transparency. This advisory supports the proposed attributed illustration; it is not a legal guarantee for every possible use or a transfer of all rights in the depicted artwork.

The [CIA gallery](https://www.cia.gov/legacy/headquarters/kryptos-sculpture/) displays Sanborn's 1988 copyright notice for Kryptos. Government hosting alone should not be treated as a blanket license for every image or depicted artwork. The [Gillogly gallery](https://voynich.net/Kryptos/) expressly reserves rights. The artist portfolio supplies no general image license in the inspected page. Linking these sources is the conservative publication choice.

[Elonka's media page](https://elonka.com/kryptos/media/) advertises two high-resolution CIA photographs, krypto20.jpg and krypto21.jpg, with a CIA credit requirement and no use charge. However, direct retrieval of both advertised image URLs returned an HTML CAPTCHA interstitial, not image bytes. No CAPTCHA was completed and no alternate route was used to evade the restriction. Their pixels, dimensions, metadata, overlap and independence were not inspected. They are acquisition leads, not acquired evidence. A prior 1991 video route had the same kind of access problem and was not retried here.

## Smithsonian acquisition path

The [individual folder record](https://www.si.edu/object/archives/components/sova-aaa-sanbojim-ref198) identifies **Jim Sanborn papers, Series 8, Box 11, Folder 34, Kryptos, undated**, record AAA.sanbojim_ref198. It is open for research, with original-paper access by appointment at the Washington, D.C. Research Center. The folder row in the [Series 8 finding aid](https://www.aaa.si.edu/collections/jim-sanborn-papers-22298/series-8) has a request checkbox, but no public folder image-viewer link was found. The thumbnail elsewhere on the Smithsonian record links to the collection, not a verified image inside this photograph folder.

The current finding aid also identifies potentially relevant, unverified containers:

- Box 12 Folder 19, Negatives, undated; Box 12 Folder 20, Transparencies, undated.
- The 2026 addition includes Box 20 Folder 5, Works, circa 2013, and Box 20 Folder 11, Negatives, undated. Their descriptions do **not** establish Kryptos content.
- [Series 9 Box 16 Folder 2](https://www.aaa.si.edu/collections/jim-sanborn-papers-22298/series-9), Kryptos Sculpture scrapbook, circa 1975–1993, may contain useful photographic references. It is not a substitute for original camera frames.

The previously inspected [artist draft page 27](https://ids.si.edu/ids/iiif/AAA-AAA_sanbojim_4129149/full/full/0/default.jpg) describes 1990 courtyard photography using 4 × 5 and 35 mm cameras, with roughly 200 exposures and around 150 slides. This is the reason to ask about surviving original frames and sleeves. It does not establish that all frames survive or that any archive container is that session.

The [current official reproduction policy](https://aeon.aaa.si.edu/rights-reproductions/) directs requests through Aeon using the desired container checkbox and Reproduction button. It limits a new-scanning order to 10 items. Posted rates are $25 per image for private research, $75 per image for publication/website use, and $30 per folder for research PDF photoduplication. A folder PDF is not promised to include full-resolution scans of slides or negatives. New-digitization turnaround is stated as within four weeks after agreement/payment; staff approval, condition, restrictions and capacity apply. The rates are published guidance, not a quote or an order placed here.

**Recommended next step:** ask first for an item inventory/contact sheet, format and frame identifiers, and a quote for a small selected set of overlapping courtyard photographs. Request a separate research master and website-use permissions; the policy describes website files as screen-optimized, which may not meet calibration needs. Do not select ten nearly identical publicity views merely to fill an order. Preserve margins, slide mounts, sleeves and verso notes; ask whether any capture-date or sequence information survives. Obtain costs and rights before committing.

No external message, account creation, reproduction order, payment, visit booking or permission acceptance was performed. The accompanying request is an unsent draft.

## Deliverables and stopping condition

This report, the source inventory and the observation-annotation JSON are suitable for repository incorporation. Photographic originals and raw page captures are research working files, not a bulk republication package. For the Site, use only the separately identified LOC illustration under its cited advisory, or link to the sources.

The next optical fitting step remains blocked on unambiguous, distributed non-K4 aperture-to-projection identities and a separately justified geometric model. The earlier proposed 12-pair train/holdout gate is not passed. No fit, date search or cipher conclusion was created in this acquisition review. Better access to the photograph series, a carefully documented field image set, or an independent annotation effort is needed before changing that status.

Coverage was bounded to public primary archive/artist/CIA routes and directly relevant photographer galleries. Four Exa searches requested 27 result slots, with duplicates and irrelevant pages excluded; two Firecrawl searches and targeted source reads supported the inventory. This is not evidence that no other relevant photographs exist.
