## Main lesson: compatibility is not identifiability

Study063 recovered the complete compatible sets correctly, but **not one of 624 planted plaintexts was unique**. Each had8–126 plaintexts. Every plant had a same-key alternative differing only at the first symbol and converging to the identical hidden state immediately afterwards. Looking at all candidates,63 cases varied only at that symbol,424 more only within the pre-crib prefix, and137 also at later unknown positions.

That distinguishes a structurally hidden initial-state degree of freedom from ambiguity elsewhere in the message. It also shows why successful roundtrip and plant inclusion do not establish unique recovery. Do not choose among such candidates using plausible English and call that a solved cipher. In063 the real target had no compatible tuples, so there are no candidates to rank.

Before a large benchmark for a future stateful model, seek a **two-trace certificate**: two distinct plaintexts under explicitly allowed key/state settings that produce the same ciphertext and satisfy the same observed clues. Independently re-encrypt both traces. This exposes non-identifiability early rather than after hundreds of ambiguous controls. It is standard validation, not a novel algorithm, and does not replace complete-set tests or null controls.

For a future genuinely new model, first state the objective: exact exclusion, recovery up to an explicit equivalence class, or unique plaintext recovery. Analyze state/key aliases and clue-mask observability early. Use independent planted controls to measure ambiguity and local-filter versus global-reachability failures. If recovery is intrinsically impossible under the available observations, say so before promising it. This is a methodological admission requirement, not permission to rerun these closed models.
