# Deliberate spelling: evidence and prior coverage

24 October 2026 UTC. This is a local source review. It does not establish a K4 transformation or authorize publication of the new investigation.

## What is actually attributed

- [Jim Sanborn’s 20 January 2005 WIRED interview](https://www.wired.com/2005/01/questions-for-kryptos-creator/) contains the short phrase “rife with mistakes on purpose.” This supports intentional mistakes generally. It does not supply a reusable spelling operation or independently identify the intent of every anomaly.
- [Dunin’s FAQ](https://elonka.com/kryptos/faq.html) specifically attributes the intentional IQLUSION and UNDERGRUUND forms to Sanborn and emphasizes their position/orientation. This is a participant’s attribution, not a newly recovered direct recording. It does not provide an executable position trigger. The FAQ labels its last modification 13 July 2020.
- [The mirrored 2005 NPR transcript](https://elonka.com/kryptos/mirrors/npr/2005transcript.html) presents PHOAR-style phonetics as narrator-reported solver speculation. It is not Scheidt’s disclosed encoding algorithm. No such spelling rule is adopted here.

The three page reads succeeded with HTTP 200 through Firecrawl; [retrieval metadata and hashes](../results/SPELLING-CORRECTION-032-SOURCES.json) distinguish cached content from retrieval time. Raw pages are kept outside the repository. No full copyrighted scrape is republished.

## Exact strings and different stages

[Existing controls](../data/controls.json) preserve IQLUSION, UNDERGRUUND and DESPARATLY. The first two are the only intention-attributed seeds in this study. DESPARATLY versus conventional DESPERATELY is conditional evidence: its minimum correction includes both A→E and an inserted E. Intent for this particular spelling is not established by the general WIRED statement. The user’s MISTSKE example is illustrative, not another monument observation.

[The existing construction audit](../independent/2026-10-22/construction/REPORT.md) independently checks worksheet/cipher equivalences: K1 can arise from a key-entry-stage alteration while the worksheet plaintext is conventionally spelled; the K2 worksheet and inscribed ciphertext differ at the relevant cell. These do not uniquely locate the historical production stage or establish intent from pen marks. They weaken the assumption that every anomaly came from one pre-encryption respelling operation. The [2006 K2 correction](https://www.elonka.com/kryptos/CorrectedK2Announcement.html) concerns an omitted **ciphertext S**, with a restored endpoint; it is not a third lexical spelling example. Its restoration does not remove UNDERGRUUND. K3’s terminal Q is not silently treated as an orthographic error.

## Prior coverage and novelty limits

- [PHONETIC-SUBSET-001](PHONETIC-FEASIBILITY.md) tested W→V and word-final D/G rules under its exact boundary assumptions. [PHONETIC-CL-001](PHONETIC-CL-FINDINGS.md) tested a fixed CL→KL adaptation, its many-to-one ambiguity and inherited periodic exclusions. Neither selected a general intentional-error trigger.
- [ONE-EDIT-028](ONE-EDIT-028-FINDINGS.md) tested 5,071 counterfactual ciphertext channels and explicit crib-index interpretations. It did not infer a systematic semantic correction channel. Its ambiguity/negatives are not reused as a proof against contextual misspelling.
- At Scott’s pinned commit `3ce19e3ae164a54af8743222ad3de5f6a93f6faf`, [NOTES.md](https://github.com/scott-dunphy/kryptos-k4/blob/3ce19e3ae164a54af8743222ad3de5f6a93f6faf/NOTES.md) reports tests of misspelling words/letters, EQUAL/QUAE and related keyed alphabets. It also reports 1,125 combination keys and 2,242 keyed alphabets, with specific mask/phase/error allowances. These source reports were read, not executed or locally reproduced. They are prior key-material coverage, not evidence of a general correction mechanism. Its stronger chart-intent conclusions are not adopted here.

The new work is exact source-consistency/identifiability analysis, including all unchanged letters and all minimum edit scripts. It is not a claim to have first considered misspelling keys, and it does not rerun those prior key sweeps.
