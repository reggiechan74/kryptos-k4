Before the train arrived, the station clerk opened the register and counted the parcels that had been left beside the office door. A traveller with a small leather case asked whether the afternoon service would stop at the village beyond the river. The clerk consulted an old timetable, then offered a cup of tea while they waited for a reply. Outside, a workman painted the fence and a child watched sparrows gathering crumbs beneath a bench. None of them knew that rain had washed away part of the road during the night. When the driver finally entered the room, everyone paused to hear what he had seen along the route. The account was ordinary, but its details mattered to the people whose journeys depended on it.
