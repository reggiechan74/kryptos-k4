# Kryptos: reconstructing the designer’s working vocabulary

Research checked 18 October 2026. This is a source-led learning and experiment plan, not a claim to have reconstructed K4. The aim is to reproduce operations Sanborn could actually perform and distinguish evidence about composition from later hints and interpretation.

## Recommendation

Build a small, exact, pencil-and-paper workshop before expanding K4 searches. Reproduce the keyed alphabet/tableau of K1 and K2 and the two rectangular rotations of K3. Then test a bounded set of reversible ways to combine those operations with fractionation, masking, and spatial reading. Require full round trips and independently specified rules. Biography should prioritize experiments, never excuse a failed decryption.

The strongest evidence supports an artist learning adaptable procedures from an expert. It does not identify a secret historical syllabus, a particular undisclosed cipher, or a psychological shortcut to K4.

## Evidence grades

- **D:** Direct statement or object/document evidence close to construction.
- **R:** Retrospective statement by a participant. Strong evidence that the statement was made, weaker evidence for exact remembered dates or procedures.
- **T:** Third-party transcription or reproduction of primary material. Preserve its provenance and transcription risks.
- **I:** Our inference or proposed experiment. Not an assertion about Sanborn’s intention.
- **U:** Unsupported by the sources examined.

“Primary” does not mean infallible. Do not collapse statement date, event date, later transcription date, and subsequent editing into one timestamp.

## Historical boundaries and unresolved dates

1. **1967 onward:** Archaeology and inscription-related art are documented background, not proof of a specific ancient cryptosystem.
2. **1988:** The NSA chronology records approval of Sanborn’s proposal. This dates project authorization, not the final plaintext or every encryption step. [NSA chronology, p. 2](https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/cia-kryptos-sculpture/doc_2.pdf)
3. **Construction through 1990:** The 1991 account explicitly allows changes while building. Use a pre-dedication cutoff for available technologies, but do not require every relevant idea to have existed in the first proposal.
4. **Egypt conflict:** Keep both oral-history and later-clue dates in structured metadata, with an unresolved flag. A recollection discrepancy is not itself a numerical key.
5. **Berlin:** The November 2025 report supplies a later explicit identification. No Berlin visit by Sanborn was established by the sources examined. The event reference must not be converted into an invented travel itinerary.
6. **Post-Kryptos art:** Compare subsequent work as evidence of recurring procedures or motifs. Do not assume it independently proves what was already present in 1988–1990. Date each artwork and each retrospective claim.

The linked 2025 letter scan was identified but could not be retrieved in this pass: [DocumentCloud source linked by Scientific American](https://www.documentcloud.org/documents/26229389-adobe-scan-nov-12-2025/). Treat exact wording beyond the accessible report as unverified here. The report itself contains an apparent “79”/“97” inconsistency; use independently verified 97-letter K4 data, not that typo.

## What to learn, ranked by evidential support

These rankings describe relevance to a learning plan, not numerical probabilities of K4’s construction.

### Tier A: directly anchored operations

1. **International Morse and segmentation.** Transcribe dots, dashes, separators, orientation, and physical gaps independently. Distinguish encoding from secrecy: a readable code alphabet can teach the viewer a mode of attention without being an encryption algorithm. The construction letter explicitly names Morse.
2. **Keyed Vigenère / periodic polyalphabetic substitution.** Learn mixed alphabets, repeating keys, index conventions, and the distinction between alphabet ordering and running keyword. Reconstruct the actual tableau before using any library labeled “Vigenère.”
3. **Rectangular transposition and composition.** Learn row/column routes, rotations, ragged grids, and inversion of permutations. Treat the photographed K3 worksheets as the strongest opportunity to reconstruct hand actions.
4. **Substitution/transposition diagnostics.** Compute frequencies, index of coincidence, repeated n-grams, and digram adjacency. Learn which statistics an operation preserves. These are validation tools, not evidence that the artist calculated them.

### Tier B: historically available, useful experiments, no attested lesson

5. **Caesar shifts and simple substitution.** A brief baseline for understanding keyed alphabets and why visible language statistics survive.
6. **Coordinate/fractionation systems:** Polybius-style coordinate representation, Bifid/Trifid-style splitting and recombining, and digraphic systems such as Playfair. These give concrete, reversible meanings to hiding ordinary single-letter statistics. None is established as Scheidt’s K4 choice.
7. **Double transposition and substitution–transposition combinations.** Test a small operation grammar with explicit route and key constraints. Do not let a solver freely permute 97 positions.
8. **Grilles, stencils, acrostics, and null/concealment systems.** Useful physical models only when tied to a reproducible extraction rule. “Visual,” “mask,” and “hidden” are insufficient to establish a grille.
9. **Semantic codebooks / agreed meanings.** Learn the difference between recovering readable words and understanding their intended reference. Keep this interpretive layer outside the character-level success metric.

For period instructional material, use the [NSA Friedman publications catalogue](https://www.nsa.gov/Helpful-Links/NSA-FOIA/Declassification-Transparency-Initiatives/Historical-Releases/Friedman-Documents/Publications/) and [*Military Cryptanalysis*, Part IV: Transposition and Fractionating Systems](https://www.nsa.gov/Portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/friedman-documents/publications/FOLDER_263/41761079080022.pdf). They provide a practical historical vocabulary. **There is no evidence in this research that these were Sanborn’s assigned books.** Later public declassification does not imply public availability to him at a particular date.

### Tier C: background literacy, low-priority search families

10. **Vernam/one-time-pad and machine cryptography concepts.** Useful to understand why a free keystream can manufacture any desired plaintext. Such freedom makes an unfalsifiable K4 “solution,” unless the keystream is independently recovered.
11. **DES and public-key-era ideas.** “Contemporary” should not automatically be read as a modern computer cipher. DES certainly predates Kryptos: [NIST’s original FIPS 46 record dates to January 15, 1977](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/46/final). Availability alone is weak evidence for use. Do not prioritize DES/RSA implementations over the directly evidenced paper procedures without a new constraint.

## Reproducible workshop: K1–K3 before K4

This is a proposed curriculum, not a claim about the sequence of Sanborn’s actual lessons.

### Lab 0: establish immutable inputs

Keep separate representations for sculpture transcription, normalized letters, physical rows/columns, punctuation, and each documented correction. Give each a checksum and provenance. Never “fix” spelling inside canonical data. Record whether a question mark is a delimiter, a visible symbol, or an enciphered token in each experiment.

Pass condition: two independent transcriptions agree, or every disagreement is logged at an exact position.

### Lab 1: recover K1’s exact alphabet arithmetic

Use alphabet `KRYPTOSABCDEFGHIJLMNQUVWXZ` with indices 0–25 and repeating keyword `PALIMPSEST`. Encryption is addition of plaintext and key indices modulo 26 in that alphabet; decryption subtracts. Verify against the opening ciphertext `EMUFPHZLRF` and plaintext `BETWEENSUB` before processing the complete canonical K1. The NSA matrix records the same alphabet and keyword.

Pass condition: decrypt then re-encrypt all canonical characters exactly. A different result from an ordinary A–Z Vigenère tool is a convention bug, not new evidence.

### Lab 2: reproduce K2 and its correction handling

Use the same alphabet and keyword `ABSCISSA`. Keep the as-inscribed and corrected endpoint cases as distinct fixtures. Document key advancement at punctuation and any restored character; no silent shifting or whitespace-driven indexing.

Pass condition: all accepted letters round-trip; each variant’s differences are explained by a named input correction. The exercise teaches how a tiny editing change can alter downstream alignment without requiring a new cipher.

### Lab 3: reproduce the physical K3 workflow

Use the 336-letter K3 plaintext, including its accepted anomalous spelling and terminal `Q`, excluding the displayed separator `?`.

1. Write it left-to-right into **8 rows × 42 columns**.
2. Rotate the entire rectangle 90 degrees clockwise.
3. Read left-to-right, top-to-bottom and reshape that stream into **24 rows × 14 columns**.
4. Rotate this second rectangle 90 degrees clockwise.
5. Read left-to-right, top-to-bottom to obtain the K3 ciphertext.

The ordering above was computationally checked in this research pass: its output begins `ENDYAHROHN` and ends `STTRTVDOHW`, matching the known ciphertext route. Preserve a full canonical comparison as a repository test before using it downstream.

Source: [worksheet images and reconstruction](https://www.thekryptosproject.com/tjp/observations/k3_method.php). The photographs are object evidence; the page’s K4 extrapolations are commentator hypotheses. Do not inherit its claims about K4 boundaries, invisible layers, or sleeve markings as established facts.

Pass condition: the full permutation and its inverse match every character, and a paper reconstruction agrees. Name dimensions as rows × columns to prevent the common width/height ambiguity.

### Lab 4: learn the diagnostic limits

Apply each candidate family to fresh, unrelated English samples of K4’s length. Measure which frequencies and positions survive. Use synthetic cases with held-out keys to test recovery. Calibrate English scores against shuffled controls and account for the number of trials. A short attractive fragment is not a result.

Pass condition: a proposed statistic reliably distinguishes the family under realistic sample sizes, or is recorded as non-diagnostic.

### Lab 5: a bounded designer-style experiment

Choose at most one new operation beyond validated A-tier primitives per experiment. Specify it before looking at the output. State what observable clue motivated its parameters, which crib positions it predicts, what would falsify it, and how to invert it. Require exact satisfaction of all independently confirmed cribs plus coherent remaining plaintext and full ciphertext regeneration.

Pass condition: another person can run the documented method without the experimenter’s interpretive choices. No post-hoc letter deletion, arbitrary anagrams, flexible date picking, or unconstrained per-position masks.

## Claims to keep explicitly unsupported

- Scheidt definitely taught a particular named K4 cipher.
- A “matrix code” necessarily means a Hill cipher or numerical linear algebra.
- A statistical mask is necessarily a physical stencil, or its order relative to encryption is known.
- A preference for little mathematics proves the algorithm is trivial.
- Every typo, font irregularity, or material is intentional and operational.
- Egyptian interests imply hieroglyphic plaintext; script-based artworks imply multilingual K4.
- A later sculpture supplies a key simply because it looks related.
- A theme-matching plaintext establishes a solve without an independently reproducible forward encoding.

## Suggested repository records

Store claims as data with `claim_id`, `claim`, `speaker`, `source_url`, `statement_date`, `event_date_or_range`, `grade`, `retrieved_at`, `transcription_status`, `supports`, `does_not_establish`, and `conflicts_with`. Keep interpretation separate from facts.

For each experiment record the exact input version, operation list, all parameters, source motivation, preregistered rejection rule, test count, complete output, crib checks, round-trip status, and negative controls. This makes learning the creator’s tools cumulative while preventing biographical storytelling from becoming an unlimited search space.
