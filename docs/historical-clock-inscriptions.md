# Urania World Clock: orientation and historical-inscription check

Research date: 18 October 2026. This preserves a completed report supplied by
the research worker. The worker directly inspected the photographs described
below; the coding executor did not independently inspect or retrieve them during
this documentation update. Links point to external sources. No images or local
inspection crops are uploaded or attached to this repository by this update.

**Correction, 18 October 2026:** the parent research worker independently
re-inspected the 28 March 1972 photograph and corrected its middle lower-panel
line to **HANOI**. The earlier report incorrectly recorded SHANGHAI there. That
superseded transcription remains in [the original documentation commit](https://github.com/reggiechan74/kryptos-k4/blob/2d4b1bca916ce9c3795029b9e9f787be436a4a72/docs/historical-clock-inscriptions.md);
the current dated reading is corrected below. This coding executor did not
independently re-inspect the photograph. Readings from 1984 and 2015 are separate
dated observations and have not been changed by this correction.

## Result

The precise panel intersected by a true 67.5-degree ENE ray from the clock's
center remains unverified. The full 1989 inscription on the relevant candidate
panel also remains unverified. Modern inscriptions cannot be projected back to
1989: dated photographs directly show differences, including before 1990.

A 24-sided regular drum gives 15-degree spacing between adjacent panel normals,
but that alone does not establish its rotation relative to north. The rotating
hour numerals cannot supply this missing calibration. An observer standing ENE
of the clock looks toward WSW and sees its ENE-facing side; an observer walking
ENE toward it approaches its WSW-facing side.

### 30 August 1989, German Federal Archives, Erwin Schneider

[External photograph and catalogue entry](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bundesarchiv_Bild_183-1989-0830-028,_Berlin,_Fernsehturm,_Weltzeituhr.jpg).
Accession: Bild 183-1989-0830-028. Attribution: Bundesarchiv, Erwin Schneider.

This photograph directly establishes CHABAROWSK on one upper panel and MAGADAN /
SACHALIN on the adjacent panel. It is a tight crop and does not show the
candidate KRASNOJARSK or IRKUTSK panels or their lower city lists. It therefore
does not answer the full requested 1989 inscription question.

The CHABAROWSK-only upper panel also differs from the 1972 WLADIWOSTOK-only
image and the 2015 combined CHABAROWSK / WLADIWOSTOK panel. Pre-1990 photographs
must therefore be individually dated; a generic pre-restoration photograph is
insufficient to establish 1989.

## Independent contextual sources — supplied review

The [official Berlin tourism description](https://www.berlin.de/en/attractions-and-sights/3561749-3104052-world-clock.en.html)
distinguishes the rotating hour ring and describes restoration changes to city
names and zone allocation.

The [clock's own organization](https://www.weltzeituhr-berlin.de/en/urania-worldtimeclock)
says it originally had 80 city names and has 146 names today.

A [contemporary 12 December 1997 report](https://www.berliner-zeitung.de/article/20-staedtenamen-wurden-bei-der-restaurierung-ergaenzt-frisch-poliert-die-weltzeituhr-dreht-sich-bald-7079)
says 20 names were added and zone allocations changed during restoration.

The reported counts cannot be reconciled into a complete original-to-current
list merely by subtraction. The photographic changes should take precedence
over an assumed unchanged historical list.

## Narva museum model: separate object, not an installed-clock substitute

The [Haus der Geschichte / LeMO 360-degree model page](https://www.hdg.de/lemo/bestand/360grad/360gradobjekt-weltzeituhr-modell.html)
identifies accession **1995/09/0108**, credits Stiftung Haus der Geschichte and
photographer **Axel Thünker**, and names Erich John as designer. Its narrative
says VEB Narva gave the model to Honecker for his 60th birthday in **1972**;
its place/time metadata separately dates the object to the GDR in **1968**.
Both fields were directly read from the museum page during this correction.
They are retained as different catalogue statements, not silently reconciled
into a date for the installed clock's panels.

The supplied independent visual review describes a **48-image rotation** of
the model, with **Peking / Manila on the upper ring and Perth below**. That
layout differs from the installed clock photographed in 1972. The coding
executor did not inspect all 48 images, and no rotation images are uploaded
here. A gift model, even with dated museum provenance, cannot substitute for
the installed clock's full **1989** inscription or fix its true-ENE orientation.

The parent also reports that a designer's **1968 sketchbook** has no geographic
orientation. Its specific source is pending; this remains an attributed lead,
not a newly verified source, calibration, or basis for choosing a city/key.
Exact true-ENE orientation and the full 1989 candidate panel remain unresolved.

## Evidence needed to close the gaps

1. A north-referenced as-built/restoration drawing labeling a fixed panel, or a
   survey/on-site photograph that simultaneously establishes the true compass
   ray and named panel, would fix the mounting angle. A clearly readable
   compass-rose photograph tied to a known panel could help, provided the rose
   itself is verified to be north-aligned.
2. A complete, dated 1989 photograph of the KRASNOJARSK and IRKUTSK panels,
   including their lower halves, or a dated plate inventory, would establish the
   historical inscriptions.
3. Relevant leads already identified: Bundesarchiv's original negative/contact
   sheet holdings for Bild 183-1989-0830-028; Landesdenkmalamt Berlin monument
   record 09020854; and the 1997 restorer Hans-Joachim Kunsch's records. These
   are leads only. No inquiry was sent and no purchase made.
